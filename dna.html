Project overview

Summary

In my New Media and Technology for Social Change class at Engineering and Science University Magnet School, I used gel electrophoresis to determine the length of a specific DNA strand and see if the length is determined by race. The process starts by receiving a tube with 50 μl extraction buffer in it (everyone labels the tube with their initials to make sure not to mix them up). Then I collected cheek cells by gently scraping the inside of my cheeks 3-4 times with a flat-end toothpick and subsequently transferred the cheek cells to the tube with a buffer by dipping the end of the toothpick in the X-TractTM buffer and swirling the toothpick to dislodge the cells. Next I incubated my tube for 10 minutes at 95°C (203°F) Once I removed my tube from heat I had to immediately use the DNA extract for PCR because the DNA extract is only stable for up to two hours, but the sooner you use it for PCR the better. Then I added 12 μl of D1S80 Primer Mix, 12 μl of 2X EZ PCR Master MixTM and 2 μl of my DNA sample with a total volume of 26 μl (0.026ml or ~0.006tsp). Then I capped the tube and ensured the reagents mix well and flicked the tube to ensure proper mixing and placed the tube inside the miniPCR machine. A few days later, I should have been able to observe how far the multiplied DNA traveled through ultraviolet light and how long the strand of DNA was. Unfortunately my batch’s samples were contaminated so the results were inconclusive.

What I learned

In this project, I learned how to multiply DNA and use gel electrophoresis to determine the length. This project also required critical thinking, reasoning, and troubleshooting skills. During the process, I added twice the amount of D1S80 Primer Mix. While I did not have the means of solving this problem, I don’t know how it affected my results because everyone sharing the batch with me had inconclusive results despite having done theirs correctly. Another problem that my group had was that we were missing the wire for the electrophoresis circuitry so I found a paperclip that we were able to use to complete the circuit. However, I think that my circuit was unused and disassembled in favor of another team’s circuit. The results of the experiment indicated that my DNA was unnaturally short. However the whole batch had that problem, thus I reasoned that the whole batch was probably contaminated due to the chances of everyone having unnaturally short DNA being so unlikely.
